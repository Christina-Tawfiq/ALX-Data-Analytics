# 📊 Part 2: Data Modeling & Interactive Reporting

## 📌 Overview

The second stage focuses on transforming raw operational data into an interactive Power BI reporting solution.

This phase introduced multi-table data modeling, relationship management, geographic analysis, and interactive dashboards that enable decision-makers to explore water accessibility challenges across Maji Ndogo.

**Goal: transform connected datasets into actionable visual insights.**

---

## 🗂️ Building the Data Model

A multi-table Power BI data model was created using the Maji Ndogo Water Services dataset.

The model integrates data from multiple sources, including water infrastructure, queue activity, pollution testing, crime incidents, and geographic locations.

Key tasks completed:

- Imported and connected multiple datasets.
- Corrected table structures and relationships.
- Resolved many-to-many relationship issues.
- Configured filter directions.
- Validated data types.
- Built a reporting model capable of supporting cross-table analysis.

### Analysis Outcome

The completed model enabled interactive filtering across all provinces of Maji Ndogo:

- Sokoto
- Kilimani
- Hawassa
- Akatsi
- Amanzi

This structure provided the foundation for all subsequent dashboards.

---

## 🌍 National Overview Dashboard

### Visuals

- Province Shape Map
- Rural vs Urban Population Distribution
- Population Served by Water Source
- Water Sources by Town
- Water Source Counts

### Key Insights

- Approximately **64%** of the population lives in rural areas, while **36%** lives in urban areas.
- Shared taps serve the largest proportion of residents across Maji Ndogo.
- Wells remain the most common water source type.
- Rivers continue to provide water access in several communities.
- Water accessibility differs considerably between provinces and towns.

### Analysis Outcome

The dashboard provides an interactive national view of water access patterns and allows users to compare service conditions across Sokoto, Kilimani, Hawassa, Akatsi, and Amanzi.

---

## ⏳ Queue Analysis Dashboard

### Visuals

- Average Queue Time per Day
- Average Queue Time per Hour
- Average Queue Composition
- Total Time Queued per Province
- Interactive Province Map

### Key Insights

- Saturday records the highest average queue times, reaching approximately **250 minutes**.
- Queue activity peaks during morning and evening collection periods.
- Women represent the largest proportion of people waiting in queues.
- Queue duration increases significantly as population demand grows.
- Sources serving more than **3,000 people** experience the longest waiting times.
- Kilimani experiences the highest overall queue burden among provinces.

### Analysis Outcome

High-demand locations place considerable time burdens on residents, demonstrating that physical access to water does not always translate to convenient access.

---

## 🧪 Pollution Analysis Dashboard

### Visuals

- Pollution Distribution Map
- Water Quality Composition Chart

### Key Insights

- Chemically contaminated sources account for approximately **40.8%** of water quality records.
- Biologically contaminated sources account for approximately **30.9%**.
- Only about **28.3%** of tested sources are classified as clean.
- Pollution levels vary considerably between provinces.
- Akatsi displays a distinctive pollution pattern, with chemical contamination representing a larger share of pollution cases.

### Analysis Outcome

Water quality challenges extend across all provinces and require targeted intervention strategies based on local contamination patterns.

---

## 🚨 Crime Analysis Dashboard

### Visuals

- Crime Types by Gender
- Victim Distribution
- Crimes by Province
- Crimes by Hour
- Crimes by Day of Week

### Key Insights

- Women account for approximately **64%** of reported victims.
- Women are nearly twice as likely to be victims of water-related crime compared to men.
- Harassment is the most common crime type.
- Theft ranks second among reported incidents.
- Sexual assault represents a major safety concern.
- Friday and Saturday experience the highest crime activity.
- Kilimani and Akatsi report the highest crime levels.
- Amanzi shows comparatively lower crime exposure for women.

### Analysis Outcome

The analysis highlights the connection between water accessibility and public safety, particularly for women who face significantly greater risks while collecting water.

---

## 💡 Key Insights

- Shared taps have the greatest population impact despite being fewer in number than wells.
- Queue duration is strongly linked to population demand.
- Water contamination remains a major challenge across Maji Ndogo.
- Crime associated with water collection disproportionately affects women.
- Geographic analysis reveals significant regional inequalities between Sokoto, Kilimani, Hawassa, Akatsi, and Amanzi.
- Effective planning requires understanding accessibility, water quality, infrastructure reliability, and public safety together.

---

## ✅ Part 2 Takeaway

This phase transformed multiple disconnected datasets into a unified analytical model that provides meaningful insights into water accessibility, infrastructure conditions, queue pressure, pollution, and public safety.

By combining interactive dashboards with geographic analysis, decision-makers can better understand where interventions are needed and prioritize resources based on impact.

The dashboards developed in this phase established the foundation for executive reporting, budgeting, and improvement planning in the next stage of the project.
