# Maji Ndogo Water Services Analysis - Part 1

## Overview

This project was completed as part of the ALX Data Analytics Program.

The objective of this phase was to explore water accessibility challenges across Maji Ndogo and identify patterns related to population distribution, water source usage, infrastructure failures, queue behavior, and crime incidents associated with water collection activities.

The analysis was conducted using Power BI and focused on transforming raw operational data into clear, actionable visual insights.

---

## Business Problem

Access to clean and reliable water remains a significant challenge across Maji Ndogo.

Stakeholders needed a better understanding of:

- How populations access water.
- Which water sources serve the largest populations.
- Where infrastructure problems are concentrated.
- Queue conditions at public water sources.
- Safety risks associated with collecting water.

---

## Dashboard Pages

### 1. National Overview

#### Visuals

- Rural vs Urban Population Distribution
- Population by Water Source Type

#### Key Findings

- Approximately **64%** of the population lives in rural areas.
- Approximately **36%** of the population lives in urban areas.
- Shared taps serve the largest proportion of the population.
- Wells represent the second most important source of water access.
- A portion of the population still relies on rivers, indicating areas with limited access to improved water infrastructure.

---

### 2. Broken Infrastructure Analysis

#### Visuals

- Broken Infrastructure by Province Map

#### Key Findings

- Infrastructure failures are not evenly distributed across provinces.
- Certain provinces exhibit significantly higher concentrations of broken water infrastructure.
- Geographic visualization helps identify priority areas for maintenance and repair initiatives.

---

### 3. Queue Analysis

#### Visuals

- Average Queue Time per Day
- Average Queue Time per Hour
- Average Gender Composition in Queues
- Queue Time vs Population Served

#### Key Findings

- Saturday recorded the highest average queue time, reaching approximately 250 minutes.
- Queue durations were considerably lower during weekdays.
- Queue activity increases during early morning and evening periods.
- Women represent the largest proportion of individuals waiting in queues across most days.
- Queue times increase substantially as the number of people served rises.
- High-demand water sources serving over 3,000 people experience the longest waiting times.

---

### 4. Crime Analysis

#### Visuals

- Total Crimes by Type
- Crimes by Province and Type
- Crime Distribution by Victim Category
- Crime Count by Day of Week

#### Key Findings

- Harassment is the most frequently reported crime associated with water collection activities.
- Theft ranks as the second most common crime type.
- Sexual assault represents one of the most significant safety concerns.
- Kilimani and Akatsi display the highest levels of reported crime incidents.
- Female victims account for the largest proportion of reported incidents across most crime categories.
- Friday and Saturday experience the highest crime volumes, while Sunday records the lowest.
